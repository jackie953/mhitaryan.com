A Dutch clean-beauty brand planned to expand across Europe in two years. **Sweden was first.** It’s one of the most developed sustainability markets in the world—high potential and unforgiving. Getting Sweden wrong would waste budget, close retail doors, and slow the whole EU plan.

I used multilingual research, cultural insight, and on-the-ground stakeholder work to replace assumptions with evidence. Then I turned that evidence into Swedish messaging and a step-by-step launch plan.

**Research shaped the message. The message shaped the channels.**  
**Strategic intelligence, not guesswork → communications that travel.**

## Who This Is For

Brands entering a new market where **culture, trust, and channels** decide outcomes: beauty/wellness, food, lifestyle, fashion—any category where **proof beats posture**.  
Leaders who want **research that writes the message** and **communications that match how buyers and consumers actually decide**.

## The Ask

“Help us launch in Sweden—adapt our messaging, choose the right channels, and build buzz.”

**The usual way:** translate the site, hire influencers, pitch national chains, hope it lands.  
**Our way:** understand the market first, then build the message and channels from what the research proves.

## What Was at Stake

- **Positioning.** Claims that worked in the Netherlands could read as “too much” in Sweden.  
- **Channels.** Without buyer priorities and store realities, pilots stall and doors stay shut.  
- **Resources.** Tight budget and timeline. Sweden was the proof market for a two-year EU rollout. A misstep here could push everything back.

---

## Approach

**Frame the decision. Gather only what matters. Turn it into action.**

- **Decision questions.** What must be true for Swedish shoppers to choose this brand? What must buyers see to say “yes” now?  
- **Consumer insight.** What people look for (ingredients, labels), who they trust, how competitors position, what communities say.  
- **Cultural translation.** *Lagom* (“not too much”), **proof before promises**, third-party marks over self-claims.  
- **Retail landscape.** Where people actually buy (D2C, **hälsokost** “health-food stores,” urban lifestyle, pharmacy/lifestyle chains) and what each buyer type cares about.  
- **Stakeholders.** Who really moves adoption—credible creators, trusted retailers, certification bodies.

> **Bridge from research → communications:** Every finding linked straight to **positioning, wording, proof, and the order of channels**. No research for its own sake.

---

## What We Found

### Consumer framing → messaging
- “Zero-waste” and “chemical-free” felt abstract or preachy.  
- What worked: **clear benefits with proof**—“natural ingredients,” “kind to skin and planet”—plus visible third-party marks like **Nordic Swan** (*Svanen-märkt*) and vegan.  
- **So we wrote it:** short, calm lines in Swedish. Show the proof. Skip the sermon.

### Retail reality → channels
- National chains wanted **evidence it sells** before shelf space.  
- Values-aligned urban stores would trial **if packaging was in Swedish** and the positioning was clear.  
- Pharmacy buyers looked for **credible results**, not eco-vibes alone.  
- **So we sequenced:** learn fast online and with a few good stores, then approach bigger doors with results—not a slide deck.

### Cultural tone → voice & visuals
- Dutch directness + activist language risked reading *för mycket* (“too intense”).  
- Swedish shoppers expect **understated confidence**: clean design, clear benefits, let the product speak.  
- **So we adapted:** Swedish minimalism in visuals, short copy, **proof before promise**.

### Timing & risk → go-to-market
- Start **D2C** to collect Swedish reviews, repeat rates, and real customer wording.  
- Add **select urban retailers** and reputable online beauty platforms to build local credibility.  
- Approach pharmacy/lifestyle chains with **evidence** (sales pace, reorders, buyer quotes).

## Example in Action

A national buyer was interested but cautious. We led with **Swedish reviews**, early repeat-purchase signals, and a clear category fit—not big claims. The meeting moved from “interesting story” to **practical placement** (where it sits, trial size, reorder logic). The difference was **evidence in their language**.

---

## Delivery

- **Positioning & narrative (SV + EN).** From “Zero-Waste Revolution” → **“Clean beauty. Natural ingredients, less fuss.”** (*Ren skönhet. Naturliga ingredienser, mindre krångel.*)  
- **Messaging & proof.** Certifications up front; Swedish ingredient transparency; simple claims like “kind to skin and planet,” with **proof before promise**.  
- **Channel sequencing.** **D2C (Stockholm/Göteborg/Malmö)** → 2–3 credible urban retailers + Swedish online beauty → pharmacy/lifestyle chains with real-world results.  
- **Community plan.** Work with Swedish sustainability/wellness creators who convert—**“show, don’t tell”** (*visa, inte säga*).  
- **Buyer pack (SV).** One-pager with basic unit economics, customer feedback, competitor gaps, operational fit.  
- **Stakeholder map.** Who to engage first to build trust quickly (and who to ignore).

---

## Impact

- **Early online traction (first 60–90 days).** Around **2–2.5%** of visitors bought; **about 1 in 3–4** bought again within 2–3 months; **120–180** Swedish reviews averaged **4.5/5**; returns stayed **under 2%**.  
- **Retail momentum.** **7–10 buyer meetings** led to **4–6 trial stores** within **6–10 weeks**. Trial sizes sold **~50–90% faster** than the category’s trial average. First reorders landed in **week 4–6**.  
- **Ready for national chains.** Real reviews, repeat purchase, and store results moved talks to **placement and facings**—keeping the EU rollout on schedule.
